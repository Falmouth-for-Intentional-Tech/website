## What we're advocating for

These recommendations are adapted from [Schools Beyond Screens](https://schoolsbeyondscreens.com/reform-proposals).

{{< grid cols="1" >}}
{{< box class="row" num="1" title="Fewer devices in the early grades" >}}
- Eliminate all device use for PK–2.
- Eliminate 1:1 device use for grades 3–8, with limited device use through a computer cart or lab model where it is appropriate for the curriculum.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="row" num="2" title="Safer devices and apps" >}}
- Block student access to YouTube, as well as all gaming, video-streaming and social media, on student devices.
- Require all apps used for instruction or on school-issued devices to be ad-free.
- Disable student access to any generative AI tools, apps, websites and software on all school-issued devices until this technology has been proven safe, legal and effective, and until age-appropriate guidelines are established.
- Require explicit parental consent for each app that collects data, per FTC guidelines on COPPA.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="row" num="3" title="Screen-free parts of the day" >}}
- Prohibit all student device use during passing periods, lunch and recess.
- Prohibit screen time from being used as a reward, incentive or behavioral reinforcement for students.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="row" num="4" title="Transparency and choice for parents" >}}
- Be transparent about technology use in schools, both on devices and in teacher-led instruction.
- Create clear pathways for parents and students who wish to opt out of device-based instruction, without penalty or reprisal, and provide low-tech alternatives.
- Require all EdTech products to meet independent standards for efficacy, and to prove that any benefits outweigh potential risks.
{{< /box >}}
{{< /grid >}}

## Questions we're asking

The district's website doesn't say how much screen time students have, or what's blocked on their devices. We're asking:

{{< grid cols="2" >}}
{{< box class="tint" eyebrow="Platforms" >}}What is each platform used for, and what's the evidence it helps?{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" eyebrow="Screen time" >}}How much daily screen time do students have at each grade?{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" eyebrow="Blocking" >}}Which sites and apps are blocked on student devices?{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" eyebrow="Phones" >}}What are the phone policies, and how are they enforced?{{< /box >}}
{{< /grid >}}

## How Falmouth compares with the evidence

Falmouth has had a full 1:1 Chromebook program since the 2015–16 school year. Every student gets a district Google account, and grades K–8 have [digital citizenship lessons](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning/digital_citizenship) from Common Sense Media. Here's how the district's stated approach compares with the [research LAUSD reviewed](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1aUGrwgNFKvCq3CBmO3dzCC3kKnOenpx3/view):

| Current approach in Falmouth | What the evidence suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Technology "elevates learning," and every student has had a Chromebook since 2015. ([Technology dept.](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/departments/technology); [Digital Learning](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning)) | Handing out devices rarely improves achievement on its own. Gains are modest, and appear only when technology supports teacher-led instruction. ([Fairlie & Robinson, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.5.3.211); [Pane et al., 2015](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RR1365.html); [Roschelle et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858416673968); [Zheng et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316628645)) |
| Technology should be "routine" and "readily available for the task at hand." ([Instructional Technology](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning/instructional_technology)) | Screen time adds up across the school day and homework. More than about two hours a day in total is linked to poorer attention, sleep and well-being. ([Twenge & Campbell, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pmedr.2018.10.003); [Sanders et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-019-0881-7); [Leonhardt et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.childyouth.2025.108508)) |
| Using technology should be "second nature" for students. ([Instructional Technology](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning/instructional_technology)) | Use should be limited and purposeful, with the strictest limits for the youngest students. ([Madigan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327); [Leonhardt et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.childyouth.2025.108508)) |
| Recommended websites and typing practice begin in kindergarten. ([Digital Learning](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/cms/One.aspx?portalId=6135966&pageId=7403846)) | Young children are the most vulnerable to unsupervised or passive screen time. ([Madigan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327); [Cerniglia et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1476718X20969846)) |

<p class="small">Studies are as cited in the <a href="https://drive.google.com/file/d/1aUGrwgNFKvCq3CBmO3dzCC3kKnOenpx3/view">LAUSD Office of Research and Program Evaluation review</a>.</p>

{{< box class="dark big" >}}
**We aren't asking Falmouth to give up technology.** We're asking the district to start from the evidence: use screens where they clearly help, and limit them everywhere else, especially in the early grades.
