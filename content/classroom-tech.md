## What we're advocating for

These recommendations are adapted from [Schools Beyond Screens](https://schoolsbeyondscreens.com/reform-proposals).

{{< grid >}}
{{< box num="1" title="Fewer devices in the early grades" >}}
- Eliminate all device use for PK–2.
- Eliminate 1:1 device use for grades 3–8, with limited device use through a computer cart or lab model where it is appropriate for the curriculum.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box num="2" title="Safer devices and apps" >}}
- Block student access to YouTube, as well as all gaming, video-streaming and social media, on student devices.
- Require all apps used for instruction or on school-issued devices to be ad-free.
- Disable student access to any generative AI tools, apps, websites and software on all school-issued devices until this technology has been proven safe, legal and effective, and until age-appropriate guidelines are established.
- Require explicit parental consent for each app that collects data, per FTC guidelines on COPPA.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box num="3" title="Screen-free parts of the day" >}}
- Prohibit all student device use during passing periods, lunch and recess.
- Prohibit screen time from being used as a reward, incentive or behavioral reinforcement for students.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box num="4" title="Transparency and choice for parents" >}}
- Be transparent about technology use in schools, both on devices and in teacher-led instruction.
- Create clear pathways for parents and students who wish to opt out of device-based instruction, without penalty or reprisal, and provide low-tech alternatives.
- Require all EdTech products to meet independent standards for efficacy, and to prove that any benefits outweigh potential risks.
{{< /box >}}
{{< /grid >}}

## What's happening in our schools

Here's what Falmouth Public Schools says publicly about classroom technology:

{{< grid >}}
{{< box class="tint" title="The district's starting point" >}}
The [Technology department's mission](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/departments/technology) says that technology "not only serves but elevates learning."
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" title="Technology woven into daily lessons" >}}
The district's [Instructional Technology page](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning/instructional_technology) aims for technology use that is "routine and transparent" and always available for the task at hand. At its best, the page says, using technology should be "second nature" for students and teachers. Teachers are pointed to the SAMR model for planning technology-integrated lessons.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" title="1:1 Chromebooks since 2015" >}}
Falmouth piloted a 1:1 Chromebook program at Morse Pond School in 2014–15 and expanded it to Grade 5. A state Digital Connections Partnership Grant paid for wireless upgrades, and the district moved to a full 1:1 Chromebook program in the 2015–16 school year ([Digital Learning page](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning)).
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" title="Google accounts for every student" >}}
Students get district-managed Google Workspace (G Suite) accounts for email, file storage and shared documents, which they can use from any internet-connected device.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" title="Digital citizenship lessons" >}}
Since fall 2019, students in grades K–8 have had [digital citizenship lessons](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning/digital_citizenship) from Common Sense Media's curriculum. The lessons cover six themes, including media balance and well-being, privacy, and cyberbullying. In spring 2020, Falmouth became the first Massachusetts district to earn Common Sense District Recognition. Students can also take a self-paced "Tech Driver's Ed" course and test to earn a "Digital Driver's License."
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" title="Recommended websites for students" >}}
The Digital Learning department [lists dozens of websites](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/cms/One.aspx?portalId=6135966&pageId=7403846) for students, starting in kindergarten. They include reading and audiobook sites, typing practice from grade K, coding sites, digital art tools and movement videos such as GoNoodle, with a separate list for upper elementary and middle school.
{{< /box >}}
{{< /grid >}}

<p class="note"><strong>Not on the district's website:</strong> how much daily screen time students have, or which sites and apps are blocked. Those are among the questions below.</p>

## How this compares with the evidence

<blockquote class="quote-vs">"Technology not only serves but elevates learning."<cite>Falmouth Public Schools, <a href="https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/departments/technology">Technology department mission</a></cite></blockquote>

Falmouth's stated goal is technology that "elevates learning," used routinely and readily available until it's "second nature." The [research LAUSD reviewed](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1aUGrwgNFKvCq3CBmO3dzCC3kKnOenpx3/view) points the other way:

| Falmouth's approach | What the evidence suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Technology "elevates learning." ([Technology dept.](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/departments/technology)) | Handing out devices rarely improves achievement on its own. Gains are modest, and appear only when technology supports teacher-led instruction. ([Fairlie & Robinson, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.5.3.211); [Pane et al., 2015](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RR1365.html); [Roschelle et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858416673968); [Zheng et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316628645)) |
| Technology should be "routine" and "readily available for the task at hand." ([Instructional Technology](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning/instructional_technology)) | Screen time adds up across the school day and homework. More than about two hours a day in total is linked to poorer attention, sleep and well-being. ([Twenge & Campbell, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pmedr.2018.10.003); [Sanders et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-019-0881-7); [Leonhardt et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.childyouth.2025.108508)) |
| Using technology should be "second nature" for students. ([Instructional Technology](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/academics/digital_learning/digital_learning/instructional_technology)) | Use should be limited and purposeful, with the strictest limits for the youngest students. ([Madigan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327); [Leonhardt et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.childyouth.2025.108508)) |
| Recommended websites and typing practice begin in kindergarten. ([Digital Learning](https://www.falmouth.k12.ma.us/cms/One.aspx?portalId=6135966&pageId=7403846)) | Young children are the most vulnerable to unsupervised or passive screen time. ([Madigan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327); [Cerniglia et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1476718X20969846)) |

<p class="small">Studies are as cited in the <a href="https://drive.google.com/file/d/1aUGrwgNFKvCq3CBmO3dzCC3kKnOenpx3/view">LAUSD Office of Research and Program Evaluation review</a>.</p>

{{< box class="dark big" >}}
**We aren't asking Falmouth to give up technology.** We're asking the district to start from the evidence: use screens where they clearly help, and limit them everywhere else, especially in the early grades.
{{< /box >}}
