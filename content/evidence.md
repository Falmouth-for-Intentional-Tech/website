## What the research says

When Los Angeles Unified wrote its new screen time policy, its Office of Research and Program Evaluation [reviewed the evidence](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1aUGrwgNFKvCq3CBmO3dzCC3kKnOenpx3/view). Their key findings:

{{< grid cols="3" >}}
{{< box num="1" title="Devices alone don't raise achievement" >}}
Benefits show up when technology is built into teacher-led instruction, not when it replaces the teacher. Programs that substitute for teacher interaction show null or mixed results.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box num="2" title="Younger children are the most vulnerable" >}}
Unsupervised or passive screen time carries more risk for preschool and early elementary students, and having an adult involved helps protect language and self-regulation.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box num="3" title="Distraction is built into the platforms" >}}
Autoplay, algorithmic feeds and multitasking are linked to weaker attention and can undercut the learning a device is meant to support.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box num="4" title="School screen time adds up" >}}
There's no exact safe limit, but total daily screen time above about two hours is associated with poorer attention, sleep and well-being. Time on school devices and homework counts toward that total.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box num="5" title="Take-home devices raise equity concerns" >}}
Families differ in how closely they can supervise home use, so unrestricted take-home devices can widen gaps between students.
{{< /box >}}
{{< /grid >}}

{{< box class="dark big" >}}
**The bottom line:** rules should be strictest for the youngest students and loosen gradually with age, and districts should keep measuring what their technology actually does for students.
{{< /box >}}

## What other districts are doing

### The bigger picture

{{< grid >}}
{{< box class="tint" eyebrow="Massachusetts" title="Phone-free schools are close" >}}
The state Senate (38–2, July 2025) and House (129–25, April 2026) have both passed bills requiring bell-to-bell, phone-free schools. The two versions differ, and since May 2026 the final bill has been in a conference committee ([S.2581](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S2581); [Turning Life On's update](https://www.fournorms.com/organizations/turning-life-on/library/tlo-where-massachusetts-stands-phone-free-schools-and-social-media)).
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="tint" eyebrow="Nationwide" title="Big districts are pausing AI" >}}
New York City and Los Angeles have paused student use of AI ([K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/a-summer-of-ai-bans-in-schools/829862/)). Turning Life On is asking for the same pause in every Massachusetts school, and has a [petition](https://www.fournorms.com/campaigns/massachusetts-hq-pause-ai-in-all-massachusetts-schools) you can sign.
{{< /box >}}
{{< /grid >}}

For more, see Turning Life On's [Learn page](https://www.turninglifeon.org/learn.html).

### District examples

{{< grid cols="2" >}}
{{< box class="accent" title="Cambridge, MA" tag="Policy in place" >}}
Cambridge Public Schools has [published grade-by-grade limits](https://www.cpsd.us/administration/information-communication-technology-services/healthy-habits-and-tech-limits). Students in PreK–2 generally don't have individual screen-based devices, and grades 3–5 have only limited access. Screens are generally not allowed at lunch or recess through 8th grade, and devices aren't used as rewards or free-time activities. Upper grades build in off-screen breaks every 20 to 30 minutes, and the district is adding evening cutoff times for digital homework.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="accent" title="Los Angeles Unified, CA" tag="Policy in place" >}}
The country's second-largest district adopted a [Student Screen Time Policy](https://edtech.lausd.org/apps/pages/screentime) built on the research above. It blocks student access to YouTube, social media, streaming and non-approved games. It limits take-home devices before high school and makes them opt-in for families. It also gives parents the right to see how much time their child spends on devices, and asks teachers to put non-screen homework first.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="accent" title="Worcester, MA" tag="Petition under review" >}}
Parents petitioned to remove individually assigned devices from preschool through 2nd grade. On November 12, 2026, the school committee [sent the petition for further review](https://rock929rocks.com/2026/11/16/worcester-parents-say-they-want-less-screen-time-for-students-in-early-school-grades/) by its teaching and learning subcommittee.
{{< /box >}}
{{< box class="accent" title="Boston, MA" tag="Petition" >}}
Parents organized by Schools Beyond Screens are [petitioning the Boston School Committee](https://jamaicaplainnews.com/2026/10/05/petition-implores-boston-school-committee-to-set-student-screen-time-policy/691798) for a district-wide screen time policy. It would remove devices from early education through 2nd grade, move grades 3–8 to shared carts and labs, and set grade-level limits.
{{< /box >}}
{{< /grid >}}
